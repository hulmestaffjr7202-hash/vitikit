🌡️ Growing Degree Days: the grower's calendar

Grapevines don't read calendars; they accumulate heat. One GDD°C = one degree above 10 °C for 24 h. The single-sine method (Snyder 1985) integrates a sine curve between daily min and max — it correctly credits cold nights that a simple average misses.

  • Bud break ~20 GDD after baseline start · flowering ~250 · véraison ~1000 (varies by variety ±15%)
  • Sprays for powdery mildew are timed by phenology, not dates — bloom is the most critical window
  • Track your own site for 2–3 seasons: every block has its own signature

Source: Coombe, B.G. (1995) "Growth stages of the grapevine"; Snyder, R.L. (1985) single-sine equation.

🗺️ Winkler regions in a warming world

Amerine & Winkler classified California into five heat-sum regions in 1944. Seventy years later, Jones et al. (2010) measured zone shifts of 10–60 km/decade poleward. Practical takeaways:

  • Region II sites today behave like old Region III — revisit variety blocks planted 20+ years ago
  • Night-time minimums matter for acid retention: Winkler ignores them; pair with mean January (or July) temps
  • New plantings: use 30-year climate normals + 2040 projections, not last decade alone

🧴 Spray math that passes audit

The label rate is per hectare or per 100 L; your tank is neither. Three numbers to always record: tank volume, product amount, water rate (L/ha). Regulators and processors increasingly require electronic spray records — keep them from day one.

  • Calibrate the sprayer annually with water only: output × speed = applied L/ha
  • Rotate FRAC groups — powdery mildew resistance is the industry's silent tax
  • Respect PHI (pre-harvest interval) on every block map you keep

❄️ Radiation frost vs advective frost

Radiation frosts happen on calm, clear, dry nights when the sky "steals" ground heat — protectable with wind machines and sprinklers. Advective frosts (cold air mass moving through, windy) cannot be fought; only site selection wins those. Our checker scores the radiation type.
